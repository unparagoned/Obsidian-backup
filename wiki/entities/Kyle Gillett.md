## Positions / claims relevant to this vault

Fadogia agrestis. His dosing scheme is the one recorded on [[Fadogia Agrestis]]. From the Huberman episode page: a "300 milligrams a day" dose is described as the level with no adverse effects in studies, with alternatives of

>600 milligrams every other day or 600 milligrams three times a week—often Monday, Wednesday, Friday
>https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/dr-kyle-gillett-tools-for-hormone-optimization-in-males

He says Fadogia can raise GGT and alkaline phosphatase, so cycle or dose less often if not monitoring bloods. The "no adverse effects" studies are rat studies; there is no human trial (see [[Fadogia Agrestis]]).

Tongkat ali. Same source: 300-1,200mg/day, standardised for eurycomanone, "no reason to cycle", lowers SHBG in people with high SHBG. General stance (WHOOP podcast): the "7 pillars of health" matter more than any supplement or drug.
https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/podcast-210-optimizing-hormone-health-with-dr-kyle-gillett/

## Credibility notes

Credentials are real but general: family medicine and obesity medicine, not endocrinology. No peer-reviewed research on Fadogia or tongkat ali found under his name; his ResearchGate profile lists "clinical research and supplementation" without fetched publications.
https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Kyle-Gillett

Conflict of interest: his practice sells hormone therapy and supplements directly, so recommendations of supplements and "optimisation" carry a commercial interest. Podcast dosing advice is expert opinion, not trial data.

Published criticism: no formal published critique of Gillett himself was found in this search. Criticism in circulation targets the Huberman/Gillett promotion of Fadogia agrestis as an under-researched herb with only rodent safety data (see [[Fadogia Agrestis]] and [[Testosterone supplements]]). Unverified: no regulatory actions or complaints found.
