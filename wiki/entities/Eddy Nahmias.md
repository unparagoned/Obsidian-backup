#freewill

**Summary:** Experimental philosopher behind the studies showing lay intuitions about free will are largely compatibilist — the empirical backbone of [[Free will]].

**The user's assessment:** "Really good academic on free will, does the studies showing people have compatibilist intuitions."

Studies on people, describing free will in different ways, suggest their intuitions are compatibilist in nature.

If people mean free will in terms of natural powers, but the media and others talk about how free will in terms of supernatural powers doesn't exist, then lay people may start to deny their natural powers.

Libet and other studies are designed so there is no preplanning, they command people not to act with conscious planning. Free will is about important decisions, that you consciously think about and deliberate — e.g. what career to pursue, not pressing a button. (The same two lines appear on [[Free will#Libet]].)

Lots of studies or details about where people might give up some of their free will unconsciously or consciously, e.g. Milgram with strong shocks. We don't always know the factors that control us. Hypothetical situations are unlikely or impossible.
