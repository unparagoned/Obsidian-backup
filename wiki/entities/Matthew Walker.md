# Learning and memory

Source: "Using Sleep to Improve Learning, Creativity & Memory" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F9KrZd_-ge0

![[raw/assets/3f9b38857cfca4f269ab05b54120eb3d_MD5.png]]

Sleeping beforehand allows the hippocampus to make better connections when learning.

Non-REM sleep: the sleep spindles are related to capacity to make new memories. They move memories from hippocampus to long term storage, which means the hippocampus is emptied and available to make new memories.

Caffeine might be able to help with learning, as it works on the hippocampus.

Sleep translocation of fact-based learning — deep NREM sleep, slow waves and spindles. Moves memories from hippocampus (short term) to cortex (long term memory).

Non-declarative memory / procedural skill / motor memory — memory replay, 10-20 times faster.

REM sleep procedural skill slows down to 50%.

Lots of spindles in the early morning, so that's where most learning is.

When you exercise — more deep sleep and less REM sleep.

When being told you will wake up early, say 5am, your body will start releasing cortisol earlier.

# Mood and emotional regulation

Source: "Improve Sleep to Boost Mood & Emotional Regulation" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_SrHS8FvMM

![[raw/assets/8368f9d931d6a5cd1e1ddc6109b37bd8_MD5.png]]

When there's not enough sleep the amygdala makes you more emotional and gives you less control.

Alcohol and cannabis impair REM sleep.

People with PTSD have higher levels of adrenaline while sleeping and at base level. Normally sleep strips memories of emotion and adrenaline levels would be low. Not getting deep NREM sleep that controlled anxiety levels.

Good sleep:

- Regularity
- Active/Exercise
- Temperature — cool (a warm bath or shower beforehand can help you cool down)
- Avoid alcohol/THC
- Sunlight in the day and dark in the evening

Sleep deprivation can make you more reward sensitive, so you eat more, do vices, etc. This is why it might work as a treatment for depression in some people. Circadian misalignment is one of the biggest factors in depression — see [[Depression]].

# Dreams

Source: "The Science of Dreams, Nightmares & Lucid Dreaming" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jy4rJcYmtUM

![[raw/assets/f584e1905b931f65ef927f1c1f8a9e9e_MD5.png]]

Rats die after 40 days of lack of REM sleep, but 60 days of no NREM sleep.

In REM sleep, cortex activity looks similar to when you are awake.

PGO waves were linked to rapid eye movements.

Dreaming of bad events helps you get over bad events. Those that didn't dream of those things recover less from those things. So dreaming of specific things helps you with those things.

It's not clear if lucid dreaming is bad or not. Brain activity during lucid dreaming might be different, could prevent brain recovery during REM.

There are some aspects like memory that require sleep that night; there is no catching up by sleeping the night after.
