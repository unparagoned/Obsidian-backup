Attribution is **not settled**. Byline Times' account attributes it to Bach; other coverage of the same thread presents this and the climate line as Epstein's. The spelling errors ("shold", "socieity") are characteristic of Epstein's emails rather than Bach's prose, which is a hint, not proof. https://bylinetimes.com/2025/12/05/how-epstein-channelled-race-science-and-climate-culling-into-silicon-valleys-ai-elite/

## 3. Fascism as governance — Bach, in fuller form

The note's fragment corresponds to a longer line quoted from the file:

>Fascism makes romantic doo-gooders like me very uncomfortable (I visited KZ Buchenwald five times and it had a profound influence on me; we East Germans inoculated ourselves very thoroughly against fascism)

Read whole, the sentence is Bach distancing himself from fascism, not endorsing it — the note's clipped version ("Probably the most efficient and rationally stringent way of governance… it makes romantic do-gooders like me very uncomfortable") inverts the emphasis. Coverage does also report Bach describing fascism as "probably the most efficient and rationally stringent way of governance" in the same discussion, so both halves appear to be his.

# Bach's response

Bach published his own account and does not dispute that the emails happened.

>I do not believe (and have never said) that people of different races have different cognitive abilities: every ethnic group contains highly capable individuals.

He says he was probing Epstein's worldview rather than endorsing it, and frames the development claim as an AI-derived hypothesis about training data rather than a claim about race and intelligence. He told the Boston Globe: "Race is NOT causal for differences in development, and race is NOT a determinant of IQ in children or adults", and that senior academics had recommended Epstein as a funder and assured him Epstein was reformed.

https://joscha.substack.com/p/on-the-jeffrey-epstein-affair

Nafeez Ahmed (Byline Times) contests that account, arguing the emails go beyond speculative neuroscience.
