#coq10 #supplements

**Summary:** Coenzyme Q10 (ubiquinone) is a mitochondrial electron-carrier and antioxidant made in most tissues; levels fall with age and with statins. The strongest use is as an add-on in chronic heart failure; statin muscle pain and depression have positive but contested meta-analyses; exercise benefit is small at best.
**Evidence:** Good evidence, modest effect (heart failure adjunct); Mixed evidence (statin myopathy, depression); Weak evidence (exercise performance); No real evidence (blood pressure, Parkinson's). Dose: 100-300 mg/day, split, with food; 100 mg three times daily in the main heart-failure RCT.
Related: [[Supplements]], [[Depression]], [[Exercise]], [[Creatine]], [[Magnesium]], [[Dementia alzheimer]]

## Heart failure
Q-SYMBIO RCT (n=420, 2 years, 100 mg three times daily on top of standard therapy): halved major adverse cardiovascular events and lowered mortality; no short-term symptom change at 16 weeks.
> The primary long-term endpoint was reached by 15% of the patients in the CoQ10 group versus 26% in the placebo group (hazard ratio: 0.50; 95% confidence interval: 0.32 to 0.80; p = 0.003) by intention-to-treat analysis. The following secondary endpoints were significantly lower in the CoQ10 group compared with the placebo group: cardiovascular mortality (9% vs. 16%, p = 0.026), all-cause mortality (10% vs. 18%, p = 0.018), and incidence of hospital stays for HF (p = 0.033).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25282031/

Meta-analysis of 32 RCTs (n=3763) in heart failure: lower all-cause mortality and HF hospitalisation (moderate-quality evidence), small ejection-fraction gain.
> RR = 0.64, 95% CI (0.48, 0.85), P = 0.002; GRADE: moderate quality
> Coenzyme Q10 reduces all-cause mortality, hospitalization for heart failure, New York Heart Association classification, and brain natriuretic peptide level and improves left ventricular ejection fraction and 6-min walk test result in those with heart failure without major adverse effects.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11515203/

## Statin muscle symptoms
Meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (n=575): less muscle pain, weakness, cramp and tiredness on CoQ10, but no change in creatine kinase.
> Compared with placebo, CoQ10 supplementation ameliorated statin-associated muscle symptoms, such as muscle pain (WMD, -1.60; 95% confidence interval [CI], -1.75 to -1.44; P<0.001), muscle weakness (WMD, -2.28; 95% CI, -2.79 to -1.77; P=0.006), muscle cramp (WMD, -1.78; 95% CI, -2.31 to -1.24; P<0.001), and muscle tiredness (WMD, -1.75; 95% CI, -2.31 to -1.19; P<0.001), whereas no reduction in the plasma creatine kinase level was observed after CoQ10 supplementation
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30371340/

## Depression #depression
Meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (n=474, 3 in medically ill patients): moderate reduction in depressive symptoms, heterogeneity driven by one study; no effect on fatigue.
> CoQ10 supplementation significantly reduced depressive symptoms compared with control (SMD: -0.68; 95% CI: -1.02 to -0.33; P <0.01; I2 =58%), with consistent results across rating scales. Sensitivity analysis indicated that heterogeneity was largely driven by a single study. No significant benefit was observed for fatigue (SMD: -0.33; 95% CI: -1.38 to 0.72; P =0.54; I2 =89%), based on only 2 trials.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41294251/

## Exercise #exercise
Systematic review/meta-analysis of 24 studies in healthy adults (2025): blood levels rise reliably, performance barely moves, certainty very low to low.
> Supplementation consistently increased blood CoQ10, indicating robust biochemical responsiveness. In contrast, performance effects were small and inconsistent. In primary analyses, chronic supplementation showed a small benefit, whereas acute supplementation showed no benefit. After excluding outliers, the chronic effect was no longer stable and the acute effect remained trivial.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41457257/

## Blood pressure, Parkinson's
NCCIH summary: no meaningful effect on blood pressure; NIH-funded trial negative in early Parkinson's.
> The small amount of evidence currently available suggests that CoQ10 probably doesn't have a meaningful effect on blood pressure.
> A major National Institutes of Health-funded study showed that CoQ10, even in higher-than-usual doses, didn't improve symptoms in patients with early Parkinson's disease.
https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10

## Dose
Linus Pauling Institute review: supplements are typically 30-100 mg/day, higher doses split.
> Doses in supplements for adults range from 30 to 100 mg/day, which are considerably higher than typically estimated dietary coenzyme Q10 intakes. [...] Doses higher than 100 mg/day are generally divided into two or three doses throughout the day (101).
https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/coenzyme-Q10

## Safety and interactions
Very high doses have been tolerated in trials; may blunt warfarin.
> There have been no reports of significant adverse side effects of oral coenzyme Q10 supplementation at doses as high as 3,000 mg/day for up to eight months (103), 1,200 mg/day for up to 16 months (65), and 600 mg/day for up to 30 months (73).
> Concomitant use of warfarin (Coumadin) and coenzyme Q10 supplements has been reported to decrease the anticoagulant effect of warfarin in a few cases (111).
https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/coenzyme-Q10

> No serious side effects of CoQ10 have been reported. Mild side effects such as insomnia or digestive upsets may occur.
https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10

## Conflicting evidence
Heart failure: NCCIH says "Research on the effects of CoQ10 in heart failure is also inconclusive." (https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/coenzyme-q10), whereas the Q-SYMBIO RCT and the 32-trial meta-analysis above report lower mortality.
Statins: NCCIH says "The overall scientific evidence does not support the idea that CoQ10 can reduce muscle pain caused by the cholesterol-lowering drugs known as statins." (same URL), whereas the 2018 JAHA meta-analysis above finds reduced symptoms. Not resolved here.

## Mechanisms
Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, which sits on the shared pathway for cholesterol and CoQ10 synthesis (LPI review).
> The use of cholesterol-lowering medications called statins can decrease circulating coenzyme Q10 concentrations.
https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/coenzyme-Q10
