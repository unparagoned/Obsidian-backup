#zinc #supplements

**Summary:** Zinc is an essential trace mineral for immune function, growth and hormone regulation. The best-supported use is high-dose lozenges to shorten a cold; effects on testosterone, depression and sleep are mostly seen in deficient or clinical populations, and long-term high doses cause copper deficiency.
**Evidence:** Good evidence, modest effect (cold lozenges); Some evidence (adjunct to antidepressants, testosterone if deficient); Weak evidence (sleep). Dose: RDA 11 mg (men) / 8 mg (women); UL 40 mg/day; lozenges 80-92 mg/day for the duration of a cold only.
Related: [[Supplements]], [[Common cold]], [[Testosterone supplements]], [[Depression]], [[Sleep]], [[Magnesium]], [[D vitamin]], [[Exercise]]

## Common cold lozenges #common-cold
Meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (n=575) of lozenges above 75 mg/day zinc found colds about a third shorter; salt type and going above ~90 mg/day made no significant difference.
> The mean common cold duration was 33% (95% CI 21% to 45%) shorter for the zinc groups.
> Five trials used zinc doses of 80–92 mg/day, common cold duration was reduced by 33%, and two trials used zinc doses of 192–207 mg/day and found an effect of 35%. The difference between the high-dose and low-dose zinc trials was not significant: 2 percentage points (95% CI: −29 to + 32).
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2054270417694291

A broader meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (n=2121, all oral zinc forms) found a shorter cold in adults but not children, with high heterogeneity and more side effects.
> Zinc shortened the duration of cold symptoms in adults (mean difference -2.63, 95% CI -3.69 to -1.58), but no significant effect was seen among children (mean difference -0.26, 95% CI -0.78 to 0.25). Heterogeneity remained high in all subgroup analyses, including by age, dose of ionized zinc and zinc formulation. The occurrence of any adverse event (risk ratio [RR] 1.24, 95% CI 1.05 to 1.46), bad taste (RR 1.65, 95% CI 1.27 to 2.16) and nausea (RR 1.64, 95% CI 1.19 to 2.27) were more common in the zinc group than in the placebo group.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22566526/

Lozenge doses exceed the UL, which the Linus Pauling Institute review says is tolerable for one to two weeks but not for longer.
> Finally, although taking zinc lozenges for a cold every two to three hours while awake will result in daily zinc intakes well above the tolerable upper intake level (UL) of 40 mg/day for adults (see Safety), the use of zinc at daily doses of 50 to 180 mg for one to two weeks has not resulted in serious side effects (117). Use of zinc lozenges for prolonged periods (e.g., 6-8 weeks) is likely to result in copper deficiency
https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/minerals/zinc

## Testosterone #testosterone
Systematic review (8 clinical, 30 animal studies) concludes deficiency lowers testosterone and supplementation raises it, with the effect depending on baseline zinc status. No pooled effect size is given, and most of the data is animal.
> After preliminary searching of papers on databases, 38 papers including 8 clinical and 30 animal studies were included in this review. We concluded that zinc deficiency reduces testosterone levels and zinc supplementation improves testosterone levels. Furthermore, the effect degree of zinc on serum testosterone may vary depending on basal zinc and testosterone levels, zinc dosage form, elementary zinc dose, and duration.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36577241/

## Depression #depression
Meta-analysis of 5 RCTs found a small reduction in depressive symptoms, mainly as an add-on to antidepressants; the effect was not significant when split by sample size.
> The standardized mean difference (SMD) showed an average reduction of 0.36 point (95%CI, -0.67 to -0.04) in the intervention group compared with the placebo group. [...] The meta-analysis by sample size (< 60 individuals and ≥ 60 individuals) did not show an effect of zinc supplementation in reducing depressive symptoms (SMD -0.28; 95%CI, -0.67 to -0.10; and SMD -0.52; 95%CI, -1.10 to 0.06).
> CONCLUSION: Zinc supplementation may reduce depressive symptoms in individuals treated with antidepressant drugs for clinical depression.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32885249/

## Sleep #sleep
Systematic review of 8 RCTs (2024): most trials reported better subjective sleep quality; no effect on sleep disorders; authors call for more trials.
> The majority of the evidence examined in this review pointed to the significant improvement effect of zinc supplementation on sleep quality in adults compared to the control groups.
> This systematic review suggests that zinc supplementation may lead to improvements in sleep quality. However, more research, primarily clinical trials, is needed to clarify the beneficial effects of zinc supplementation on sleep quality with consideration of dietary zinc intake and the Recommended Dietary Allowances of zinc (RDA) in the different populations.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11456512/

## Dose
RDA and UL per the Linus Pauling Institute (review).
> The recommended dietary allowance (RDA) for adult men and women is 11 mg/day and 8 mg/day of zinc, respectively.
> Long-term consumption of zinc in excess of the tolerable upper intake level (UL; 40 mg/day for adults) can result in copper deficiency.
https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/minerals/zinc

## Safety and interactions
Zinc blocks absorption of tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics; intranasal zinc has caused permanent loss of smell.
> Concomitant administration of zinc supplements with certain medications like tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics may decrease the absorption of both zinc and the medications, potentially reducing drug efficacy.
> Of serious concern are several case reports of individuals experiencing loss of the sense of smell (anosmia) after using intranasal zinc as a cold remedy (127). [...] Since zinc-associated anosmia may be irreversible, intranasal zinc preparations should be avoided.
https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/minerals/zinc

## Conflicting evidence
Hemilä's lozenge-only meta-analysis (7 trials, >75 mg/day) reports a consistent ~33% shorter cold and encourages use; the CMAJ all-formulation meta-analysis (17 trials) finds a benefit in adults but with 95% heterogeneity, no benefit in children and more adverse events, and says larger trials are needed before recommending. Quotes above.

## Mechanisms
Likely: lozenge benefit depends on ionic zinc released in the pharynx, which is why dose and salt form are analysed separately (see Hemilä 2017 above). Unverified beyond that source.
