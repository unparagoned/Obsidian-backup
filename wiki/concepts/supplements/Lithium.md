**Summary:** Trace lithium in drinking water is associated with lower suicide and lower dementia mortality, and low doses (5 mg/day of lithium orotate) do reach the brain measurably. The user's conclusion from the evidence: not enough good-quality studies or dosing clarity to take it yet.

**Evidence:** *Weak evidence* — ecological/observational studies for the water-lithium findings (reverse causation and confounding wide open), one small imaging study (n=9) for brain uptake. Dose: therapeutic lithium is 150-300mg carbonate; the supplement studies use ~5mg orotate.

# Suicide

Drinking-water lithium levels are linked to suicide rates. Observational study.

>In line with the majority of findings from other countries, our results indicate that the intake of lithium with drinking water may have a gender-dependent suicide-protective effect.
>https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34800573/

# Dementia

Linked to lower dementia levels, at very low water concentrations.

>Association between trace-Li levels and a lower risk or mortality from dementia were observed at concentrations of Li in drinking water as low as 0.002 mg/L and 0.056 mg/L.
>https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39212809/

Video (neurologist reviewing the evidence and brands): "Low-Dose of This Mineral REVERSES Dementia?" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h0b3EfuKaYQ

![[raw/assets/7f63ad9fbffc3d6926642aadf7fa7efc_MD5.png]]

Lithium orotate is the type that works, but there are limited studies and the optimal dosing isn't known. We get lithium from diet and the water supply, so the video's own recommendation is not to take it yet.

# Dose

Review on dose-dependent effects.

>Clinical and molecular effects of lithium are numerous, and its effects also appear to have a certain degree of specificity related to the dose administered. In sum, the clinical effects of lithium are maximal for mood stabilisation at concentrations higher than 0.6 mEq/l. However, lower levels may be sufficient for preventing depressive recurrences in older populations of patients, and microdoses could be effective in decreasing suicide risk, especially in patients with BD.
>https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38914810/

5mg shows up in the brain, and is much lower than therapeutic doses of 150-300mg. Primary imaging study (Journal of Affective Disorders).

>Lithium orotate supplements were taken by nine healthy adult male subjects (5 mg daily) for up to 28 days, providing 2–7 % of the lithium content of a typical therapeutic lithium carbonate dose. One-dimensional 7Li-images were acquired on a 3.0 T MRI scanner. All subjects were scanned on day 14 or 28; seven were scanned on both, one at baseline and one after 7-days washout.
>https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165032724008723

Results: 7Li-MR signal amplitude was broadly stable between days 14 and 28. Two subjects had notably higher 7Li signal intensities (approximately 2-4×) compared to other participants.

Limitations, as stated in the paper: lithium adherence was self-reported by all participants without formal validation. The coarse spatial resolution necessary for detection of low concentrations of 7Li exhibits imperfect spatial separation of signal from adjacent pixels.

Conclusion: 7Li-MRI performed using a clinical 3T scanner demonstrated detection of lithium in the brain at very low concentration, in the range of approximately 10-60 mM.

# The user's position

Not enough good quality studies, and the dosing amounts aren't clear, so don't recommend yet.
