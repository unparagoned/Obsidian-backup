#ginkgo #supplements #dementia

**Summary:** Ginkgo biloba leaf extract (standardised as EGb 761) is the most-studied herbal for memory. The largest trial (GEM, n=3069, 6 years) found no prevention of dementia or cognitive decline in healthy older adults. In people who already have dementia, industry-linked meta-analyses of EGb 761 at 240 mg/day report modest benefit, while Cochrane calls the evidence inconsistent and unreliable.
**Evidence:** No real evidence (prevention of dementia or cognitive decline in healthy people); Mixed evidence (symptomatic treatment of existing dementia, 240 mg/day EGb 761); No real evidence (tinnitus). Dose: 120-240 mg/day standardised extract; 240 mg/day in the positive dementia trials.
Related: [[Supplements]], [[Dementia alzheimer]], [[Sleep]], [[Depression]], [[Exercise]], [[Creatine]]

## Dementia prevention (healthy older adults)
GEM RCT (JAMA 2008): 3069 people aged 75+, 120 mg twice daily, median 6.1 years. No reduction in dementia or Alzheimer's; point estimate slightly worse.
> The hazard ratio (HR) for G. biloba compared with placebo for all-cause dementia was 1.12 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.94-1.33; P = .21) and for AD, 1.16 (95% CI, 0.97-1.39; P = .11). G. biloba also had no effect on the rate of progression to dementia in participants with MCI (HR, 1.13; 95% CI, 0.85-1.50; P = .39).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19017911/

GEM cognitive-decline analysis (JAMA 2009): no slowing of decline in any domain.
> Compared with placebo, the use of G. biloba, 120 mg twice daily, did not result in less cognitive decline in older adults with normal cognition or with mild cognitive impairment.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20040554/

NCCIH summary of the same trial.
> There was no difference between the gingko and placebo groups in the rates at which participants developed dementia of any type or Alzheimer's disease.
https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginkgo

## Treatment of existing dementia
Meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (n=2561, 22-26 weeks) of EGb 761: better cognition and daily-living scores, mainly at 240 mg/day.
> the weighted mean differences in change scores for cognition were in favor of EGb761 compared to placebo (-2.86, 95%CI -3.18; -2.54); the standardized mean differences in change scores for activities in daily living (ADLs) were also in favor of EGb761 compared to placebo (-0.36, 95%CI -0.44; -0.28) [...] All these benefits are mainly associated with EGb761 at a dose of 240 mg/day.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25114079/

Meta-analysis of 4 trials in mild dementia (n=782), 240 mg/day: positive on cognition, global, ADL and quality of life; adverse events same as placebo.
> From four eligible trials data of 782 patients with mild dementia were included in the meta-analysis.
> Adverse events were similarly frequent in patients treated with EGb 761 and placebo (p=0.66).
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12436692/

Cochrane review (2009, 36 trials): results inconsistent; three of the four most recent trials negative.
> Of the four most recent trials to report results three found no difference between Ginkgo biloba and placebo, and one found very large treatment effects in favour of Ginkgo biloba.
> The evidence that Ginkgo biloba has predictable and clinically significant benefit for people with dementia or cognitive impairment is inconsistent and unreliable.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19160216/

## Other claimed uses
NCCIH: not helpful for heart disease, blood pressure, tinnitus or MS.
> Research suggests that ginkgo is not helpful for preventing heart disease, treating high blood pressure, or relieving symptoms of tinnitus or multiple sclerosis.
https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginkgo

## Dose
120 mg twice daily in GEM (negative); 240 mg/day EGb 761 in the positive dementia meta-analyses. Quotes above.

## Safety and interactions
Meta-analysis of 18 RCTs (n=1985) on haemostasis markers: no signal of increased bleeding from standardised extract.
> Based on meta-analysis of hemostasis outcomes, comparison of mean difference or baseline change between treatment and placebo groups did not indicate a higher bleeding risk associated with standardized GBE.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21923430/

NCCIH still warns about anticoagulants and raw seeds.
> Ginkgo may increase the risk of bleeding in people who are taking anticoagulant drugs, such as warfarin.
> Fresh ginkgo seeds are toxic when consumed orally, and serious side effects have also occurred in people who consumed roasted ginkgo seeds or the crude ginkgo plant.
https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginkgo

Cochrane: side effects no worse than placebo.
> Ginkgo biloba appears to be safe in use with no excess side effects compared with placebo.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19160216/

## Conflicting evidence
Treatment of dementia: the 2015 and 2025 EGb 761 meta-analyses report consistent benefit at 240 mg/day; Cochrane 2009 reports inconsistent, unreliable evidence with publication bias not excluded. Note that the positive meta-analyses pool trials of a single manufacturer's product. Bleeding: RCT meta-analysis finds no haemostasis effect; NCCIH and case reports warn of bleeding with anticoagulants. Not resolved here.

## Mechanisms
Cochrane lists the proposed actions.
> The mechanisms of action are thought to reflect the action of several components of the extract and include increasing blood supply by dilating blood vessels, reducing blood viscosity, modification of neurotransmitter systems, and reducing the density of oxygen free radicals.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19160216/
