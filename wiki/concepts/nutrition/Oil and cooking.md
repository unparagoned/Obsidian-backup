**Summary:** Extra-virgin olive oil was the most stable oil under cooking heat in the study the note relies on, producing the fewest polar compounds, with coconut oil second — and smoke point turned out not to predict heat performance. The seed-oil-inflammation material on the page is much weaker than the stability material.

**Evidence:** *Some evidence* for the stability finding (one 2018 lab study, replicated in outline by a 2010 deep-frying study); *Weak evidence* for the seed-oil/colon-cancer link (news report of a study of tumours from ~80 patients).

Related: [[Protect Our Plates]] · [[Ingredients, brands and tools]] · [[Cooking techniques]] · [[Naturalistic fallacy]] · [[Dementia alzheimer]]

> [!warning] Conflicting evidence
> The page holds two different claims about seed oils: that EVOO is the most heat-stable oil (a lab measurement, reasonably supported) and that seed oils cause chronic inflammation and colon cancer (a news report on a small tumour study). These are not the same claim and the second does not follow from the first.

The user's practice:

- EV olive oil is best for cooking.
- Olive oil is good for brain health.
- Can use butter/ghee or coconut oil when cooking.
- Avocado oil is best for cast iron and carbon steel — it's pretty good at high temps and doesn't leave a sticky layer like EVOO.

# Heat stability

Summary of the 2018 Australian oil-stability study, via drweil.com (secondary source).

>Results showed that extra-virgin olive oil was the safest and most stable when heated to temperatures even higher than those commonly used for sautéing, deep-frying and baking. It produced the lowest quantity of polar compounds compared to the other oils tested. The runner up was coconut oil.

>The study also disproved the commonly held view that oil with a high smoke point is best suited for high-temperature cooking. In fact, an oil's smoke point doesn't indicate how it will perform when heated.
https://www.drweil.com/diet-nutrition/cooking-cookware/does-high-heat-hurt-olive-oil/

The study itself (Acta Scientific Nutritional Health 2018).

>This study reveals that, under the conditions used in the study, smoke point does not predict oil performance when heated. Oxidative stability and UV coefficients are better predictors when combined with total level of PUFAs. Of all the oils tested, EVOO was shown to be the oil that produced the lowest level of polar compounds after being heated closely followed by coconut oil.
https://actascientific.com/ASNH/pdf/ASNH-02-0083.pdf

Supporting sources listed in the video the user took this from (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l_aFHrzSBrM):

- 2018 Australian study on olive oil stability — https://actascientific.com/ASNH/pdf/ASNH-02-0083.pdf

# Seed oils

News report (LBC), repeated twice in the original note.

>Seed oils such as sunflower, grapeseed, canola, and corn have been linked to chronic inflammation, according to new research.
>The American-based study looked at tumours from over 80 people diagnosed with colon cancer - aged between 30 and 35.
https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/doctors-warn-against-dangers-of-cooking-oil-amid-links-to-colon-cancer-increases/

The note's line "Results from here line up with studies linking them to cancer" is the user's inference, not something the stability study says.
