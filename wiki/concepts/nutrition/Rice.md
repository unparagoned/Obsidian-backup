**Summary:** Two health-relevant claims plus the user's method: parboiling in excess water and draining removes arsenic from white and brown rice, and cooking rice with oil (then cooling) is claimed to cut absorbed calories by up to 70%.

**Evidence:** *Some evidence* for the arsenic reduction (the parboil-and-drain method is established); *Weak evidence* for the 70% calorie figure — it comes from a single unpublished undergraduate project widely repeated in the press.

Related: [[Cooking techniques]] · [[Resistance starch]] · [[Glucose spikes]] · [[Chemicals and microplastics]]

# Method

Quick simple:

- Fry garlic and ginger for 30 seconds in a decent amount of oil.
- Fry the rice for a minute or so in that oil.
- Add 2× ml of water (2× works for 150g of rice but might be a lower ratio for more rice).
- Bring to the boil, then simmer for 10 minutes.
- Turn off and leave for 10 minutes.

1g rice to 1ml water is a good ratio for white rice — this is half of what I do, I use 300ml of water for 150g of rice. But it doesn't take evaporation into account, so it might not be so different in the end when comparing pan vs sous vide.

Source: "How to make Perfect Rice at home, according to science" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IjjdAheuNKs

# Arsenic

Boil in lots of water for 5 min, get rid of the water, then cook with the right amount of water for the rest of the time. This gets rid of arsenic in white and brown rice.

Source: "How to reduce Arsenic in rice" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hhGjsyoHcA

# Calories

Cooking rice with oil can reduce calorie intake by 70%.

Source: "Doctor Explains How To Cut Calories In Rice!" — https://www.youtube.com/shorts/uyQ2-O4aL1s
