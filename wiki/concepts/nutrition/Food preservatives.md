#preservatives

**Summary:** One prospective cohort (Nature Communications 2025) found 13 common preservatives (12 after multiple-test correction), including potassium sorbate, sodium nitrite, citric and phosphoric acids and rosemary extract, associated with higher type 2 diabetes incidence (1,131 cases).

**Evidence:** *Some evidence* (single observational cohort; the authors themselves note epidemiological data are lacking).

Related: [[Ultra processed foods(UPF)]] · [[Ultra processed foods(UPF)#Emulsifiers]] · [[Chemicals and microplastics]] · [[Glucose spikes]]

Prospective cohort (Nature Communications 2025; Likely: NutriNet-Santé), 1,131 incident diabetes cases; observational, confounding by overall diet quality possible.
>Experimental studies suggested potential adverse effects of preservative food additives, but epidemiological data are lacking.
Thirteen (12 after multiple test correction) widely used individual preservatives are associated with higher diabetes incidence (n=1131cases): potassium sorbate, potassium metabisulfite, sodium nitrite, acetic, citric and phosphoric acids, sodium acetates, calcium propionate, sodium ascorbate, alpha-tocopherol, sodium erythorbate, and rosemary extracts. These findings call for their safety re-evaluation and support recommendations to favour fresh and minimally processed foods without superfluous additives.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67360-w
