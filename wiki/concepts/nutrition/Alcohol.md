#alcohol

**Summary:** The user's list of alcohol harms (sleep, headache, gut, weight, blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, liver, cancer, muscle growth), with WHO and World Heart Federation statements that no level is safe and a 2023 JAMA Network Open meta-analysis finding no mortality benefit from moderate drinking.

**Evidence:** *Strong evidence* of harm; *No real evidence* that moderate drinking protects the heart (WHO, World Heart Federation, 107-cohort meta-analysis).

Related: [[Sleep#Alcohol]] · [[Sleep]] · [[Heart]] · [[Dementia alzheimer#Alcohol]] · [[Gym]]

From <[https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/drinking-moderately-not-healthier-than-abstaining-rcna77604](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/drinking-moderately-not-healthier-than-abstaining-rcna77604)>

Meta-analysis of 107 cohort studies, 4.8 million participants (JAMA Network Open 2023): no mortality benefit below 25 g ethanol/day versus lifetime non-drinkers.
>This systematic review and meta-analysis of 107 cohort studies involving more than 4.8 million participants found no significant reductions in risk of all-cause mortality for drinkers who drank less than 25 g of ethanol per day (about 2 Canadian standard drinks compared with lifetime nondrinkers) after adjustment for key study characteristics such as median age and sex of study cohorts.

https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2802963

No level of alcohol consumption is safe for our health

https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/04-01-2023-no-level-of-alcohol-consumption-is-safe-for-our-health

The evidence is clear: any level of alcohol consumption can lead to loss of healthy life. Studies have shown that even small amounts of alcohol can increase a person’s risk of cardiovascular disease, including coronary disease, stroke, heart failure, hypertensive heart disease, cardiomyopathy, atrial fibrillation, and aneurysm. Studies that claim alcohol can offer protection against cardiovascular disease are largely based on purely observational research, which fails to account for other factors, such as pre-existing conditions and a history of alcoholism in those considered to be “abstinent”. To date, no reliable correlation has been found between moderate alcohol consumption and a lower risk of heart disease.

https://world-heart-federation.org/news/no-amount-of-alcohol-is-good-for-the-heart-says-world-heart-federation/
