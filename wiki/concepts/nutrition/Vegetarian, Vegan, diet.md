**Summary:** A 2025 British Journal of Cancer cohort compared meat-eaters with poultry-eaters, pescatarians, vegetarians and vegans. Vegetarians had lower risk of pancreatic, breast, prostate, kidney cancer and myeloma but higher oesophageal squamous-cell risk; vegans had higher colorectal cancer risk (HR 1.40).

**Evidence:** *Some evidence* (single prospective cohort, observational; hazard ratios mostly 0.7-0.93).

Related: [[Meat]] · [[Diet Types]] · [[Protein]] · [[B12]] · [[Gut microbiome]]

Prospective cohort (British Journal of Cancer 2025; Likely UK Biobank or EPIC-Oxford, Unverified) comparing diet groups on site-specific cancer incidence:
>Compared to meat eaters, poultry eaters had lower risk of prostate cancer (0.93, 0.88–0.98), pescatarians had lower risks of colorectal (0.85, 0.77–0.93), breast (0.93, 0.88–0.98) and kidney cancer (0.73, 0.58–0.93), vegetarians had lower risks of cancers of the pancreas (0.79, 0.65–0.97), breast (0.91, 0.86–0.97), prostate (0.88, 0.79–0.97), kidney (0.72, 0.57–0.92) and multiple myeloma (0.69, 0.51–0.93) but higher risk of squamous cell carcinoma of the oesophagus (1.93, 1.30–2.87), and vegans had higher risk of colorectal cancer (1.40, 1.12–1.75).
