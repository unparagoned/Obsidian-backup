**Summary:** The user's view: even one large early-morning coffee measurably impaired their sleep, so a cut-off of roughly 10 hours before bed may be a better rule than 'none after 11am'. Coffee also carries benefits: cohort data on lower dementia risk and lower bowel-cancer recurrence.

**Evidence:** *Good evidence* that morning caffeine reduces sleep efficiency that night (200 mg at 07:10, crossover in nine men); *Some evidence* (cohort) for lower dementia risk and cancer recurrence.

Related: [[Sleep#Caffeine]] · [[Sleep]] · [[Sleep - circadian schedule]] · [[Dementia alzheimer]]

I used to recommend no caffeine after 11am but a single large European size coffee early in the morning still significantly impacted sleep that night. Some people also suggest avoiding decaf coffee after 11am since it still contains some caffeine. But caffeine has lots of health benefits. So maybe up to three 10 hours before sleep might be a good balance. 

Placebo-controlled crossover in nine healthy men (Landolt 1995): 200 mg caffeine at 07:10 still reduced sleep efficiency and total sleep time that night.
 >To further investigate the role of adenosine in sleep regulation we administered 200 mg of caffeine at 07.10 h and analyzed the sleep stages and EEG power spectra during the subsequent night in nine healthy men. Caffeine levels in saliva decreased from a maximum of 17 μmol/l one hour after intake, to 3 μmol/l immediately prior to the sleep episode starting at 23.00 h. Compared to placebo, sleep efficiency and total sleep time were significantly reduced.
 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/000689939500040W

Cohort study (JAMA 2025): coffee/tea intake associated with lower dementia risk; observational, so reverse causation possible.
>Greater consumption of caffeinated coffee and tea was associated with lower risk of dementia and modestly better cognitive function, with the most pronounced association at moderate intake levels.
https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2844764

Normal and decaf has benefits against cancer. 

World Cancer Research Fund news post (press, summarising an observational study):
>Coffee linked to lower recurrence of bowel cancer
>https://www.wcrf.org/about-us/news-and-blogs/coffee-linked-to-lower-recurrence-of-bowel-cancer/
