#basalcognition

**Summary:** The claim that intelligence comes in degrees and does not require neurons, so brainless systems — slime mould, single cells, tissues — can be described as cognitive. [[Michael Levin]] is its most prominent advocate. The dispute is not about the physics, which nobody contests; it is about whether adaptive physical behaviour earns the word *intelligence*.

Related: [[Michael Levin]] · [[Consciousness]] · [[Physicalism]] · [[michael-levin-on-slime-mould-intelligence]]

> [!warning] Conflicting evidence
> The best-known evidence — slime mould solving mazes — was shown to be gradient-following rather than problem-solving (see [[#It's not true that maze-solving shows intelligence]]). The minimal-cognition case rests on other behaviour (habituation, memory-like effects, decisions made without exploring), and the two claims are held by overlapping authors, not opposing camps.

# The position

*Some evidence, contested framing.* Levin's definition is functional and broad: sense conditions, retain information, choose, and pursue a goal by different means. On that definition *Physarum polycephalum* qualifies without a nervous system.

> Cognition is all around you. It really is everywhere...not just for furry, brainy things.
https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2021/10/right-now-can-slime-molds-think

He is not claiming slime mould has human-like experience. The claim is that cognition is a spectrum and neurons are one implementation of it, not the criterion. The position paper is Vallverdú et al., *BioSystems* 165 (2018), which Levin co-authored — a review, not an experiment.

The experimental strand is separate and better grounded: Murugan et al. (2021), on which Levin is an author, reports *Physarum* deciding between masses it has not touched.

> able to make decisions about mass in its environment without actually exploring the environment
https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2021/10/right-now-can-slime-molds-think

# It's not true that maze-solving shows intelligence

*Debunked.* Andrew Adamatzky went through the celebrated maze-solving prototypes — slime mould, epithelial cells, navigating droplets — and showed they exploit one of two ordinary physical facts.

> We show that most prototypes utilise one of two phenomena: a shortest path in a maze is a path of the least resistance for fluid and current flow, and a shortest path is a path of the steepest gradient of chemoattractants. We discuss that substrates with so-called maze-solving capabilities simply trace flow currents or chemical diffusion gradients.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04681

What this does and does not establish: it kills **the maze test as sufficient evidence**. It does not show slime mould has no cognition, because the minimal-cognition case does not rest only on mazes.

**The twist:** Adamatzky is a co-author on the Levin minimal-cognition paper above. The demystification and the cognition claim come from the same research community, so this is not a critic-versus-advocate fight — a point the source that prompted this page got wrong.

# Levin's reply to "it's just physics"

He rejects the objection as assuming a binary, and points out that brains are physically implemented too.

> Right, aspects of physics and materials properties underlie intelligence (as is well-understood and exploited by the field of morphological computing and others). Is the claim in that video that "real intelligence" has to mean there's no physics underneath?

> The confusion in the argument in that video is caused by assuming a binary "is/is-not intelligent."
https://thoughtforms.life/qa-from-the-internet-and-recent-presentations-3/ (29 August 2025)

The counter-objection, as put by the ChatGPT summary in [[michael-levin-on-slime-mould-intelligence]] and **not sourced to any published critic**: if everything sits somewhere on the competence spectrum, the word stops discriminating, and the move "risks making 'intelligence' too inclusive to be informative".

*Unverified:* neither side, in the material read so far, states what evidence would separate minimal cognition from ordinary self-organisation. That looks like where the disagreement actually sits.
