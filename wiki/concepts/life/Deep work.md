**Summary:** Notes from Cal Newport's *Deep Work*: output = time × intensity of focus; track lead measures (hours of deep work) rather than lag measures (papers published); phone use when bored erodes the capacity to focus; write definitive emails that set times and plans.

**Evidence:** *Weak evidence* — popular book; the quoted passage is Newport's own claim, not a study.

Related: [[Memory]] · [[Routine]] · [[Tips]] · [[Justin Sung]] · [[Social media]]

High-Quality Work Produced = (Time Spent) × (Intensity of Focus)

Focus on lead rather than lag measures — e.g. count hours worked per day rather than papers published per year. Those are easier to measure and influence.

Newport's own claim, quoted from the book:

>In my experience, this analysis is spot-on. If you give your mind something meaningful to do throughout all your waking hours, you'll end the day more fulfilled, and begin the next one more relaxed, than if you instead allow your mind to bathe for hours in semiconscious and unstructured Web surfing.

Using your phone when you are bored reduces your ability to focus when you need to do deep work. You want to build your ability to focus and concentrate.

In emails, write something that's definitive and sets out times and plans; that reduces lots of missed emails.
