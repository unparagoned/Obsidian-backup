**Summary:** The user's notes from *Atomic Habits*: habit stacking (after X I will Y), plans beat decisions, environment design, one place per activity, reframing so wanted habits sound positive and unwanted ones negative, and anchoring a calm state to three breaths and a smile.

**Evidence:** *Weak evidence* — popular book, no studies cited. Implementation intentions ("I will X at time T in place P") do have a real research base, uncited here.

Related: [[Habits]] · [[James Clear]] · [[Tips]] · [[Routine]] · [[Self Control]]

- Point and talk loudly about habits, good or bad. E.g. "I don't need this cookie, it'll make me fat."
- Making a plan is key — that way you just follow the plan rather than needing to make the decision.
- Habit stacking: after X I will Y. "After I eat dinner, I will clean up the kitchen."
- I will X at time in location.
- Set up the environment to make things easier.
- Set certain places or devices for different uses. E.g. breakfast bar for eating, sofa for TV, desk for work.
- Reframe things so you think about habits you want to do positively and negative habits negatively.
- Link three breaths and smiling with something that makes you happy. Then you can do the breaths other times to make you happy.
