**Summary:** Response of blood cholesterol to dietary cholesterol is genetically variable: about a third of people are hyper-responders, and polymorphisms in apo E, apo B, apo A-IV, LPL, CETP and the LDL receptor modify the response. Apo E2 carriers are non-responders; apo B R+ carriers, especially with E4, show marked LDL rises.

# Cholesterol

Press release (Texas Biomed, animal model: baboons) on a gene driving diet-induced LDL rise.

>Scientists at the Southwest Foundation for Biomedical Research (SFBR) have found a gene that causes high levels of bad cholesterol to accumulate in the blood as a result of a high-cholesterol diet.
>https://www.txbiomed.org/news-press/news/scientists-find-gene-responsible-high-cholesterol-levels-blood-finding-animal-model-offers-hope-reducing-risk-humans/

Institutional news summary (SINH, Chinese Academy of Sciences) of a cohort study using genetic risk scores for dietary cholesterol response.

>Though many genes involve in the complex regulation on maintaining cholesterol homeostasis, few studies have systematically evaluated collective effects of multiple genetic variants on the relationships between dietary cholesterol intake and plasma cholesterol....
Overall, this study provides important evidence regarding to personalized recommendation for dietary cholesterol intake and also demonstrated potential application of genetic risk score in precision nutrition.
The study found that increased dietary cholesterol intake was significantly associated with elevated plasma TC, LDL-C and non-HDL-C levels. It also revealed that GRSTC and GRSLDL-C were positively associated with circulating levels of TC and LDL-C, respectively. Moreover, at given levels of dietary cholesterol, individuals with higher GRSTC or GRSLDL-C had higher plasma TC, LDL-C or non-HDL-C levels than their counterparts with lower GRSs.
http://english.sinh.cas.cn/news/news/202009/t20200903_242570.html

Review (Ordovas, 2000, primary literature summary) of polymorphisms affecting lipid response to diet.

>Influence of genetic polymorphisms on responsiveness to dietary fat and cholesterol
>The DNA polymorphisms on the genes encoding apo A-IV, apo B, apo C-III, apo E, lipoprotein lipase, cholesteryl ester transfer protein, lecithin:cholesterol acyltransferase (phosphatidylcholine-sterol O:-acyltransferase), and LDL receptor were found by others to be associated with the plasma lipoprotein response to dietary intervention.
>https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11063469/

Dietary intervention study (ATVB 1997) stratified by apo E and apo B genotype.

>High cholesterol intake increased LDL cholesterol levels in all genetic categories except in the apo E2 phenotype irrespective of the combinations with other polymorphisms.
In summary, in any genetic combination, individuals with the ε2 allele had the lowest LDL cholesterol values and were nonresponders to dietary cholesterol, whereas subjects with the apo B R+ allele had marked LDL elevations, especially in combination with the ε4.
https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.ATV.17.1.38
