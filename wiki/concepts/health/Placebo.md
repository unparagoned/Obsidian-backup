**Summary:** Placebo effects run through identifiable physiology, not just belief: naloxone blunts placebo analgesia (so endogenous opioids are involved), CB1 blockade does the same, conditioned placebos suppress immune markers, and what people believe they ate changes their ghrelin response.

**Evidence:** *Good evidence, modest effect* — mechanistic human studies, small samples, single findings each.

Related: [[Mental attitude]] · [[David Linden]] · [[Depression]] · [[Claims and testimony]] · [[Glucose spikes]]

# Opioid and cannabinoid systems

There are interesting studies where they can limit the placebo effect on pain relief by blocking the cannabinoid/opioid receptors, showing that the placebo effect is working through real physiological systems.

Primary study (1983) using naloxone to block placebo analgesia.

>naloxone diminished the analgesic effectiveness of the placebo, suggesting that endogenous opioids are involved in producing placebo-induced analgesia.
>https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6308540/

Review (Healthcare, MDPI 2024) on the cannabinoid pathway.

>The cannabinoid system, through CB1 receptors, has been shown to mediate pain relief, and blocking these receptors can diminish the analgesic effects of placebos.
https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/12/22/2314

# Immune conditioning

There are examples of placebo impacting the immune system. Human conditioning study.

>re-exposure to the conditioned stimulus (drink), but now paired with placebo capsules, induced a suppression of immune functions as analyzed by the IL-2 and IFN-gamma mRNA expression, intracellular production, and in vitro release of IL-2 and IFN-gamma, as well as lymphocyte proliferation.
>https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12468450/

# Mindset

Mindset impacts physiological reaction. The Crum milkshake study — same shake, different label.

>The mindset of indulgence produced a dramatically steeper decline in ghrelin after consuming the shake, whereas the mindset of sensibility produced a relatively flat ghrelin response. Participants' satiety was consistent with what they believed they were consuming rather than the actual nutritional value of what they consumed.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21574706/
