**Summary:** Prudent Reviews tested five "titanium" pans against a plain stainless pan on nonstick, scratch resistance and salt pitting. Top performers: **Viking PureGlide Pro** (cooks like nonstick, survived every scratch test) and **Hestan NanoBond** (hardest, slickest surface). **Our Place Titanium Always Pan Pro** released a dry egg with no fat at all — otherwise only new traditional nonstick does that. **Heritage Steel Titanium** cooks like ordinary stainless. **Taima** performed well but is 2mm thin and warped.

**Source quality:** one YouTube review (Prudent Reviews, ~April 2026) — hands-on testing with controlled procedure, but n=1 per pan, one tester, and every pan is affiliate-linked in the description. Not independent lab testing.

# What "titanium" actually means on each pan

The word covers four different constructions, which is the review's main point.

- **Hestan NanoBond** — stainless with an aluminium core; titanium vaporised in a vacuum chamber and bonded to the surface in a layer "as thin as human hair". Claim: 4× harder than stainless.
- **Viking PureGlide Pro** — stainless with aluminium core; titanium heated to ~30,000° and sprayed onto the cooking surface. Matte grey, looks like a nonstick coating; Viking confirmed it is neither Teflon nor ceramic. Claim: 3× harder than stainless, 5,000 pizza-cutter cycles.
- **Our Place Titanium Always Pan Pro** — stainless exterior, aluminium core, cooking surface entirely pure titanium. Textured pattern claimed to create airflow between food and pan.
- **Taima** — stainless exterior, aluminium core, pure titanium surface, textured. Claims it is impossible to corrode.
- **Heritage Steel Titanium** — 316Ti stainless, i.e. titanium in the *alloy*, not on the surface. Makes no nonstick claim; claims better resistance to salt pitting.

# Results

## Nonstick

Method: preheat to 325°F, ½ tsp oil + 2g butter, cook an egg. Then repeat with no fat. Then pancakes, with and without butter.

- **With fat** — Viking behaved like a nonstick pan (egg slid with a shake, no spatula). Taima, Our Place and Heritage Steel didn't stick either. Hestan ran hot and browned the egg but flipped cleanly. The plain stainless pan needed a spatula but released fine.
- **Without fat** — eggs stuck badly to Hestan, Heritage Steel and Taima. Viking stuck slightly but flipped intact. Our Place released a completely dry egg:

>The biggest surprise was the Our Place pan. Even though I didn't add any oil or butter, the egg did not stick… Up until now, the only pans that have done that in my testing are traditional nonstick pans, and they usually have to be pretty new for it to work.

- **Pancakes** — all fine with butter. Without butter, Viking, Our Place and Taima performed great; Hestan stuck slightly; Heritage Steel and plain stainless stuck most, but the pancakes still came out.

## Scratch resistance

Five tests: 5lb-weighted metal ladle ×50, same with a spatula, serrated steak knife with 130g of magnets ×15, Scotch-Brite green side under a 5lb weight ×20, and a stand-mixer whisk held against the surface for 30s.

- **Viking** — best. Zero scratches throughout. White marks under the ladle and whisk turned out to be metal dust that wiped off.
- **Hestan** — rub marks only; surface underneath smooth every time. The knife was noticeably quieter on it: "the surface is so smooth and hard that the knife almost glides across it."
- **Our Place** — one slight scratch mark from the knife; no damage from sponge or whisk (loud grinding noise, but nothing underneath).
- **Heritage Steel and Taima** — significant scratches from the knife and from the sponge.
- **Plain stainless** — scratches you can feel with a fingernail in most tests. A traditional nonstick pan was cut straight through by the knife.

None of the damage changed cooking performance — every pan still released an egg afterwards.

## Salt pitting

Method: 20g non-iodised salt + 20g water left in a cold pan for an hour, boiled dry, left another hour, washed. Three rounds.

All five titanium pans came through with no pitting. The plain stainless pan developed dark spots after round one that vinegar and Bar Keepers Friend wouldn't remove, and by round three they had eaten into the steel deep enough to feel.

The reviewer's own caveat, which matters:

>To be completely fair, this is an extreme test. I let salt sit in cold water in a cold pan for an hour, which is just about the worst thing you can do with stainless steel. If you add salt after the water is boiling and don't leave salty food sitting in your pans overnight, pitting shouldn't be an issue.

# Verdict

**Buy** — Viking PureGlide Pro:

>It looks like a nonstick pan, it cooks like a nonstick pan, but it didn't get damaged in any of my scratch tests… It's hard to believe the cooking surface isn't a nonstick coating, but it's not.

**Buy** — Hestan NanoBond: "lives up to its claims… slicker and more scratch-resistant than standard stainless steel", used by the reviewer for years.

**Buy for the nonstick** — Our Place Titanium Always Pan Pro: unusual-looking surface, "you can't deny the performance."

**Only if you want pitting resistance** — Heritage Steel Titanium: cooks like regular stainless, which follows from the titanium being in the alloy rather than on the surface. The reviewer prefers Heritage Steel's non-titanium Eater Series for its handle.

**Avoid** — Taima: met most of its claims but the pan is 2mm thick, and it warped and spun on the cooktop after a few uses.

General advice: read the fine print, since "titanium" describes very different constructions, and titanium is only one factor next to handle design, thickness, weight and heat performance.
