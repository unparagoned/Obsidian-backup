# The user's method

Sous vide. Cool before searing. Sear at 204°C or lower — don't need a really hot pan.

# Fallow's "ultimate steak" method

Source: YouTube, Fallow (London restaurant, two chefs) — "we put the biggest steak cooking myths to the test". Myth-busting side-by-side tests, not controlled science; the video is sponsored by Made In cookware and the pan links are affiliate. This is only the final method they assembled at 22:15 from the tests that passed — the individual myth verdicts earlier in the video aren't recorded here.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UOulv0BxM3k

**The method, in order:**

- **Sirloin, cut off the bone.** They used sirloin because it's lean and so shows the cooking fairly; bone-in lost its own test.

> We're going to take this sirloin off the bone as a first step because from our myth busting, we found that in a steak format on the bone didn't make enough difference and we thought you'd get a more even cook and a better crust off the bone. We chose sirloin for this mythbusting experiment because it's one of the leanest so it shows the cooking more sort of fairly as a controlled test.

- **Temper 20-30 minutes out of the fridge.** This is the one the chef expected to bust and didn't.

> I was very skeptical about taking the steak out the fridge 20 to 30 minutes before, but this one actually had a really profound impact. And it's quite remarkable how quickly it does come up to temp.

- **Plain salt and pepper at the pan, not a dry brine.** Dry brining failed their test; the pepper crust is a deliberate part of the result.

> So, we've gone with a classic seasoning instead of the dry brining, which we found didn't make a big enough impact. Classic seasoning was actually just much nicer, and I really love that pepper crust that we got with it.

- **Render the fat cap first, then flip every 20 seconds** — and move the steak to a fresh part of the pan each flip rather than flipping it in place, so it never sits on cooled metal.

> Rather than flipping in the same place, you want to flip it onto a cleaner, hotter section of the pan. So then that part of the pan is nice and hot while this part is cooled down. And then you keep flipping it around the pan, chasing the hot spots so your steak is always searing and not boiling.

- **Turn the heat down before basting.** Butter goes in to a cooled pan with garlic, and thyme goes in only at the end, once the bubbling has died down.

> You don't want to start butter basing in a raging hot pan because the butter will just burn.

> Ultimately, with the butter basing, what we decided was the butter base does lead to a better crust and a better flavor on the final steak. However, we did like the fact that the fresh time really came through in the infused butter.

- **Rest in the butter, on a bed of thyme leaves.** They rated this above resting on a plate.

> We also found that it was almost far superior having rested inside that butter as well.

- **Rest 10 minutes and let carryover do the last 7°C.** Out of the pan at ~43°C, finishing at ~50°C.

> Currently sitting around 43°. So what we call this stage now is carryover cooking. […] So we'll be looking for 50° when this is finished after 10 minutes.

- **Carve, then a second hit of fresh pepper and the infused thyme butter**, so there's both cooked and raw pepper on the plate.

Their tasting verdict put the finish, not the cooking, as the upgrade:

> Peppercorns and the fresh thyme is leveled this up massive.
