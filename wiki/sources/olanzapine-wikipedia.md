# Olanzapine (Wikipedia article)

**Raw source:** `raw/inbox/Olanzapine.md` — a clipping of the English Wikipedia article on olanzapine, 503 lines, 127 footnotes. The revision date is not recorded in the clipping; internal evidence (a 2023 US prescription-ranking figure) puts it no earlier than 2024.

Related: [[wiki/concepts/drugs/Olanzapine|Olanzapine]] · [[Antipsychotics]] · [[Depression]] · [[Mounjaro tirzepatide]] · [[Pharmaceutical industry]]

The substantive content has been written up on [[wiki/concepts/drugs/Olanzapine|Olanzapine]]. This page records what was checked against outside sources and where the article is wrong.

## Claims checked and confirmed

- **The 2009 Eli Lilly guilty plea.** The article's figures match the DoJ announcement of 15 January 2009 — $1.415bn total, of which a $515m criminal fine.

> Eli Lilly and Company Agrees to Pay $1.415 Billion to Resolve Allegations of Off-label Promotion of Zyprexa

https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/eli-lilly-and-company-agrees-pay-1415-billion-resolve-allegations-label-promotion-zyprexa

- **US prescription rank.** ClinCalc gives olanzapine as #167 in 2023. The "more than 3 million prescriptions" figure is in the right range (ClinCalc's 2024 estimate is 3,642,411), but the site does not show a 2023 prescription count, so that half of the sentence is unconfirmed.

https://clincalc.com/DrugStats/Drugs/Olanzapine

- **Anorexia nervosa research.** Confirmed against Attia et al. 2019 (RCT, n=152, 16 weeks, adult outpatients): weight gain favoured olanzapine, psychological symptoms did not improve. The article's summary is accurate and appropriately hedged.

## Where the source is wrong

- **The Lybalvi weight-gain figures are badly overstated.** The article claims olanzapine produces "about 9 kg (20 pounds) in the short-term, which typically increases to around 23-45 kg (50-100 pounds) following long-term use", against "roughly 5 kg (11 pounds)" for olanzapine/samidorphan. None of that matches the trial it is describing. ENLIGHTEN-2 (24-week phase 3) reported least-squares mean percent weight change of **4.21% on olanzapine/samidorphan versus 6.59% on olanzapine**, a difference of -2.38% (p=0.003) — roughly 3 kg versus 5 kg at a typical baseline weight, not 5 kg versus 9 kg. Long-term cohort data put olanzapine weight gain at about 11 kg beyond 13 weeks, with 9% of patients exceeding 20 kg over 2.5-3 years; a *typical* 23-45 kg is not supported by anything found. The corrected figures are what appear on [[wiki/concepts/drugs/Olanzapine|Olanzapine]].

https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2020.19121279
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6947810/

- **The cannabinoid hyperemesis paragraph carries no citation at all** in the article, and its comparison does not hold up. "It has a high success rate comparable to haloperidol" appears to conflate two separate literatures: the haloperidol-versus-olanzapine head-to-head is for *chemotherapy*-induced nausea and vomiting, while the haloperidol evidence in cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome comes from the HaVOC trial, which compared haloperidol against ondansetron and did not include olanzapine. Olanzapine in CHS rests on a handful of case reports.

https://www.annemergmed.com/article/S0196-0644(20)30666-1/abstract

## Unverified

- **The Leucht et al. 2013 weight-gain figure (SMD 0.74, highest of 15 antipsychotics).** The meta-analysis is real — *Lancet* 382(9896):951-962, PMID 23810019 — but the effect size sits behind a paywall and could not be read directly. The ranking it is used to support (olanzapine worst of the 15 for weight gain) is consistent with everything else checked, so it is stated on the concept page with the design named and this caveat.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23810019/

- The pharmacodynamics binding table (Ki values across ~45 targets) was transcribed as-is and none of its 20-odd primary references were checked.
- The brand-name table, dosage forms, legal-status dates, chemical synthesis and animal-toxicology sections were not checked.
