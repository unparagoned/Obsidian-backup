[Bushido: The Samurai Code of Japan: With an Extensive Introduction and Notes](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9idXNoaWRvLXRoZS1zYW11cmFpLWNvZGUtb2YtamFwYW4td2l0aC1hbi1leHRlbnNpdmUtaW50cm9kdWN0aW9uLWFuZC1ub3Rlcy1ieS1hbGV4YW5kZXItYmVubmV0dD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00MjY1ZmQxYWMmX3NzPXI=)

by Alexander Bennett

I can’t remember which subscriber emailed me about

[this book](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9idXNoaWRvLXRoZS1zYW11cmFpLWNvZGUtb2YtamFwYW4td2l0aC1hbi1leHRlbnNpdmUtaW50cm9kdWN0aW9uLWFuZC1ub3Rlcy1ieS1hbGV4YW5kZXItYmVubmV0dD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00MjY1ZmQxYWMmX3NzPXI=)

, but I really liked it. Written in 1905,

[Bushido: The Samurai Code of Japan](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9idXNoaWRvLXRoZS1zYW11cmFpLWNvZGUtb2YtamFwYW4td2l0aC1hbi1leHRlbnNpdmUtaW50cm9kdWN0aW9uLWFuZC1ub3Rlcy1ieS1hbGV4YW5kZXItYmVubmV0dD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00MjY1ZmQxYWMmX3NzPXI=)

was the first book written for a Western audience about the code of conduct that governed the lives of Japan’s ruling class. It gets to “the soul of Japan” by answering the question of why certain ideas and customs prevail. It was a huge sensation in the US when it came out. I believe Theodore Roosevelt read

[this book](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9idXNoaWRvLXRoZS1zYW11cmFpLWNvZGUtb2YtamFwYW4td2l0aC1hbi1leHRlbnNpdmUtaW50cm9kdWN0aW9uLWFuZC1ub3Rlcy1ieS1hbGV4YW5kZXItYmVubmV0dD9fcG9zPTEmX3NpZD00MjY1ZmQxYWMmX3NzPXI=)

. It’s a lovely little peon to the virtues of an ancient tradition and deserves to be read up there with

[The Book of Five Rings](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9ib29rLW9mLWZpdmUtcmluZ3MtYS1jbGFzc2ljLXRleHQtb24tdGhlLWphcGFuZXNlLXdheS1vZi10aGUtc3dvcmQ=)

and

[Zen in the Art of Archery](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy9waGlsb3NvcGh5MjQ=)

(two other favorites of mine). Fictionally, there is also

[Rules for a Knight](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cudGhlcGFpbnRlZHBvcmNoLmNvbS9wcm9kdWN0cy95YTIz)

[The Gatekeepers: How the White House Chiefs of Staff Define Every Presidency](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0JvdHQ2SQ==)

by Chris Whipple (

[Audiobook](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL2hCRmlCM2E=)

)

There are lots of books about powerful and famous people.

[This book](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0JvdHQ2SQ==)

is a fascinating look at the man behind the man–the chiefs of staff to the presidency. As it happens, I gave a talk at the US Naval Academy earlier this week and I talked about President Jimmy Carter (one of our most underrated and certainly most decent president). The big failure of Carter’s presidency (and perhaps why he didn’t get a second term) is discussed in

[this book](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0JvdHQ2SQ==)

. Carter, reacting to the more imperial presidencies of his predecessors, opted not to have a chief of staff for most of his presidency. He was more accessible as a result…but also hopelessly bogged down. You had to ask Carter personally to use the White House tennis court! Anyway, he could have done more good had he been a little more hierarchical. The first thing I did after reading

[this book](https://preview.convertkit-mail4.com/click/dpheh0hzhmh4/aHR0cHM6Ly9nZW5pLnVzL0JvdHQ2SQ==)

was ask my assistant to read it. :)
