Walnuts look like one of the best-supported single foods: big cohorts show ~14% lower all-cause mortality and ~25% lower CVD death at 5+ servings/week, and actual RCTs (PREDIMED, WAHA) back it up with fewer heart attacks/strokes and lower LDL. Only common nut with lots of plant omega-3 (ALA), plus fibre, magnesium and polyphenols.

Practical: ~30g/day (about 7 walnuts, ~200 kcal). Swap it in for a snack (biscuits/cereal bar), don't just add it on top. Plain unflavoured only — honey roasted etc is confectionery. High ALA means they go rancid fast; store in the fridge or freezer, not the cupboard.

Source video: [What Eating Walnuts Does to Your Risk of Dying - Doctor Alex](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJWEVUvRHlk) — checked the references, numbers in the video match the papers (couple of small slips noted below).

# The studies

**Walnuts and mortality (Liu 2021, Nutrients)** — NHS + HPFS cohorts, ~93,000 people, up to 20 years. Dose response: <1 serving/wk ~5% lower death rate, 1/wk ~6%, 2-4/wk ~13%, 5+/wk ~14%, and ~25% lower CVD mortality. Observational, so healthy-user confounding possible, but diet reassessed every 4 years.

>The hazard ratios for total mortality across categories of walnut intake...were 0.86 (95% CI, 0.79, 0.93) for ≥5 servings/week

>A greater life expectancy at age 60 (1.30 years in women and 1.26 years in men) was observed among those who consumed walnuts more than 5 servings/week

From <[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8401409/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8401409/)>

**PREDIMED (Estruch 2018, NEJM)** — the actual RCT. 7,447 Spaniards aged 55-80 at high CV risk, randomised to Med diet + olive oil, Med diet + 30g/day mixed nuts (half walnuts), or low-fat advice. Median 4.8 yrs. Nut group had 28% fewer major CV events (HR 0.72). Note this is the 2018 re-analysis after the 2013 paper was retracted over randomisation problems at some sites — result held up after excluding the dodgy participants.

>the hazard ratio was 0.69 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.53 to 0.91) for a Mediterranean diet with extra-virgin olive oil and 0.72 (95% CI, 0.54 to 0.95) for a Mediterranean diet with nuts, as compared with the control diet

From <[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/)>

**PREDIMED nut substudy (Guasch-Ferré 2013, BMC Medicine)** — the "~60% lower death" claim in the video comes from here, not the NEJM paper. Within PREDIMED, people in the nut arm who were already eating >3 servings/wk at baseline had the lowest mortality in the whole trial (HR 0.37, i.e. 63%). Small numbers, baseline diet is observational within the trial, so treat as suggestive.

>Participants allocated to the Mediterranean diet with nuts group who consumed nuts>3 servings/week at baseline had the lowest total mortality risk (HR 0.37; 95% CI 0.22 to 0.66)

From <[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738153/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738153/)>

**WAHA trial (Rajaram 2021, Circulation)** — 708 healthy elders aged 63-79 (Barcelona + California), randomised to 30-60g walnuts every day for 2 years vs none. (Video said 780 — it's 708.) Total cholesterol down 8.5 mg/dL (~0.22 mmol/L), LDL down 4.3 mg/dL (~0.11), small dense LDL particles (the ones that get into artery walls) down 6.1%. No weight gain despite the extra ~200 kcal/day. Interesting sex difference: LDL fell 7.9% in men vs 2.6% in women.

>The walnut diet significantly decreased (mg/dL) total cholesterol (mean –8.5), LDL-C (mean –4.3), and IDL cholesterol (–1.3)

>Total LDL particles and small LDL particle number decreased by 4.3% and 6.1%, respectively

>Body weight was stable, with mean 2-year changes of 0.06 kg in the walnut diet and –0.51 kg in controls

From <[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478315/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478315/)>

**Meta-analysis, nuts generally (Aune 2016, BMC Medicine)** — 20 prospective studies. Per 28g/day of nuts: CHD risk 0.71, CVD 0.79, total cancer 0.85, all-cause mortality 0.78. Most of the benefit is already there at ~15-20g/day, so more isn't automatically better.

>The summary RRs per 28 grams/day increase in nut intake was for coronary heart disease, 0.71 (95% CI: 0.63-0.80)... all-cause mortality, 0.78 (95% CI: 0.72-0.84)

From <[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27916000/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27916000/)>

**Nuts and CVD (Guasch-Ferré 2017, JACC)** — 210,000 people across 3 cohorts, walnuts specifically only needed 1+ serving/week to show an association.

>Consumption of peanuts and tree nuts (2 or more times/week) and walnuts (1 or more times/week) was associated with a 13% to 19% lower risk of total cardiovascular disease and 15% to 23% lower risk of coronary heart disease.

From <[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29145952/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29145952/)>

**Nuts and mortality (Bao 2013, NEJM)** — the classic one. ~119,000 in NHS/HPFS, 30 yrs. Clean dose response down to HR 0.80 at 7+ servings/wk. Note: part-funded by the International Tree Nut Council.

>0.80 (95% CI, 0.73 to 0.86) for seven or more times per week (P<0.001 for trend)

From <[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24256379/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24256379/)>

# Caveats

- Mortality numbers are all observational — walnut eaters do other healthy stuff. The RCTs (PREDIMED, WAHA) only prove hard CV events and lipids, not the mortality/life-expectancy claims.
- PREDIMED has the retraction history; the corrected estimate is still solid but the trial's credibility took a knock.
- Bao 2013 had tree nut industry co-funding.
- LDL drop in WAHA is real but modest (~4 mg/dL) — a fraction of what a statin does. It's a food, not a drug.
